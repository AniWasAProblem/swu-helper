# Wicket Ewok Swarm (v14): analysis and pilot guide

Decklist: `ewoks-v14.json`. Rule references are to `rules/SWU_Rules_v9_0.pdf` (v9.0).

This is a ground aggro deck with a backup plan of chip damage. It floods the board with cheap Ewoks. Wicket draws a card every round, and Beast Lair turns a spare card into a 3/3 Beast every round. Cloud-Rider Veteran and Red Squadron Y-Wing damage the opponent's base every round, whatever blocks them. The deck aims to win on turns 6–8 and stops resourcing at 4–5.

The deck is built to survive pings and sweepers. The best decks punish small units with repeatable 1–2 damage pings and sweepers, such as Luke Skywalker's leader action, Red Five, The Legacy Run, Pre Vizsla, Mortar Trooper and Topple the Summit. So the core Ewoks have 4 or 5 HP, not 2 or 3.

The deck has three tools against threats its units can't fight:
- **Heavy Ion Cannon:** sits on your base and deals 2 damage to a unit in either arena every phase. Sweepers can't touch it.
- **Outland Protector:** a 1-cost space Sentinel that chump-blocks a big ship.
- **Dorsal Turret:** turns a small ship into a kill for any enemy ship.

All three tools are aimed at space threats. Against big **ground** units the deck has much less: Aggressive Negotiations when your hand is full, Reckless Sacrifice, Relentless Hunters and Ima-Gun Di (5 power each), and Turning the Tide if your board is wide. Dorsal Turret can't help, because every Vehicle in the deck is a space unit and so can only attack space units.

The percentages are calculated. The piloting advice comes from the games listed in "Games played" at the end. None were played with this exact list. All were played with earlier versions that share the leader, base and most of the main deck. None of those games included Ima-Gun Di, Beast Lair or Saw Gerrera, so the advice on those three cards comes from their text and the rules, not from play.

## Decklist

**Leader: Wicket, Few Greater Battles to Fight** (`HMW_014`; Aggression / Heroism)
- **Leader side:** when a friendly unit attacks a unit that costs more than it, you may exhaust Wicket to draw a card.
- **Epic Action:** deploy him once you control 4 or more resources. Deploying doesn't spend resources; it is a threshold.
- **Unit side:** 2/5 ground. *On Attack:* if you control a unit that costs 3 or less, draw a card.

**Base: Bright Tree Village** (`HMW_023`; Command, 30 HP). It has no ability of its own. It is here for its Endor planet trait, which turns on Village Troublemaker, Adamant Ewoks and Nightfall. It also carries Heavy Ion Cannon and Beast Lair.

## Analysis

### 1. Game-stage plan

- **Early (turns 1–3):**
  - **Turn-1 plays:** 25 units cost 2 or less, so you almost always have one (98.9%). You have a 1-cost unit 72% of the time.
  - **Turn 2–3 card flow:** an Ant Droid played on turn 1 attacks a 2-cost or bigger unit on turn 2. Wicket's leader side draws when the attack is declared, and Ant Droid's When Defeated draws again if it dies in the trade.
  - **Turn 2–3 engine:** Beast Lair costs 2. Played on turn 2, it makes its first Beast at the start of round 3, and that Beast first attacks in round 4.
  - **Turn 3:** Wicket deploys, when you reach 4 resources.
  - **Space:** Outland Protector, Y-Wing and V-19 give you a space unit from turn 1–2. Against space decks, having one early matters (see "Weaknesses").
- **Mid (turns 4–6):** you play two or three cards a turn.
  - **Clear blockers:** Heavy Ion Cannon, Logray, Ewok Archers' Ambush, Reckless Sacrifice, Turning the Tide and Nightfall.
  - **Kill a big ship:** Dorsal Turret on a ready Y-Wing, V-19 or Outland Protector. The Cannon finishes a ship that has already been damaged.
  - **Kill a big ground unit:** Reckless Sacrifice deals 5. Relentless Hunters and Ima-Gun Di attack for 5. Nightfall's +2 attack or the Cannon finishes the job. Each of these kills a 5-HP unit. A 6–7 HP unit needs two of them, or Aggressive Negotiations on a ready attacker when your hand is full (see "Card-specific lines").
  - **Chip the base:** Cloud-Rider and Y-Wing, every round. Adamant Ewoks deals 1 to the base when it is played.
  - **Survive pings:** Ewok Brigade (4 HP), Archers, Logray and Chirpa (5 HP), and Cloud-Rider (4 HP) all survive a 1–3 damage ping. Logray turns each ping on a cheap friendly unit into one of yours.
  - **Keep the board stocked:** with Beast Lair out, you start each round with a fresh 3/3 if you have a spare card.
- **Late (turn 7+):**
  - **Finishers:** Nightfall (+2 power and an extra attack), Aggressive Negotiations (+1 power per card left in hand), Relentless Hunters, and the Saboteurs for getting past Sentinels.
  - **Spare cards:** the Cannon turns cards you can no longer use (a stranded Turret, a late 1-drop) into 2 damage each round, and Beast Lair turns them into a 3/3. With both out, each round you choose which one gets the spare card.
  - **Odds:** you see at least one Nightfall by turn 6 69% of the time, without counting Wicket's draws.
  - **Risk:** there are no big units, and the hand is usually only 2–4 cards by now. If the board is even on turns 8–9, the opponent's cards are better than yours. Beast Lair slows that decline by adding a 3/3 every round, but a 3/3 doesn't beat their late-game cards. This is a deck that has to win before turn 9.

### 2. Cost curve

11 / 24 / 13 / 2 cards at costs 1–4, average 2.12. That is a low, dense aggro curve, and with nothing above 4, stopping at 4–5 resources is right.

At 4 resources you can cast a 2-drop and two 1-drops, or a 3-drop and a 1-drop, every round. The 3-cost slot is V-19, Ewok Archers, Ima-Gun Di, Turning the Tide and Heavy Ion Cannon. The Cannon is the only 3-drop that doesn't affect the board when it is played, but it replaces itself with a card draw, and every later use of it removes or weakens a unit. Beast Lair is the 2-cost equivalent: nothing the round it lands, then a 3/3 every round after. Both are slow, so play at most one of them in the first three turns unless you have no unit to play.

### 3. Leader and base

- **Leader side:**
  - **Trigger:** Wicket draws whenever a cheaper unit attacks a more expensive one. That's once per round, because you exhaust him to do it.
  - **Who qualifies:** a 1-drop qualifies against anything costing 2 or more. A Dorsal Turret Y-Wing (cost 2) attacking a 4-cost piloted ship also qualifies.
  - **Beasts:** tokens count as costing 0 (3.7.2, p. 18). A Beast attacking any enemy unit that costs 1 or more qualifies, including the 1-cost units that your 1-drops can't attack up into.
  - **Pumps don't change cost:** Nightfall raises power, not printed cost.
  - **The Cannon doesn't trigger it:** the Cannon's damage isn't an attack.
- **Deploy (4 resources, turn 3):**
  - **Draw condition:** the deployed 2/5 draws on every attack while you control a unit costing 3 or less. 34 of 36 units qualify, and so does every Beast token (cost 0), so it is almost always live.
  - **Ready on deploy:** he deploys ready, even from exhausted (3.4.4, p. 15). So you can use the leader-side draw first, then deploy and attack for a second draw.
  - **Toughness:** 5 HP survives most pings. It doesn't survive two -3/-3 effects (Anakin Skywalker, Champion of Mortis), a 5-power attacker after a ping, or Topple the Summit after a ping.
- **Base:**
  - **What it does:** 30 HP and no ability. The Endor trait powers 9 cards (3 Troublemaker, 3 Adamant Ewoks, 3 Nightfall).
  - **Healing:** none. Nothing in the deck heals your base or your units, so every point of damage is permanent.
  - **Fortify upgrades:** Heavy Ion Cannon and Beast Lair attach to your base (7.5.21, pp. 34–35). The base becomes a source of damage and of fresh units that survives Wicket's defeat and every sweeper. Only an ability that defeats upgrades can remove them, such as Outer Rim Constable.

### 4. Weaknesses

- **Big ships:**
  - **Removal:** only Dorsal Turret kills a ship with more than 5 HP. That includes a Luke-piloted fighter (7/9), a Boba-piloted Justifier (8/8) or TIE Bomber (4/8), Chimaera (6/6) and Nightbrother (6/7). You have only 2 Turrets in the main deck, and each needs a Vehicle that has survived a round.
  - **Blocking:** Outland Protector and V-19 buy time. They don't kill anything.
  - **By turn 5:** you see a Protector or a Turret 74% of the time.
- **Big ground units:**
  - **Dorsal Turret is blank:** its carriers (Y-Wing, V-19, Outland Protector) are space units and can't attack a ground unit.
  - **Removal:** Reckless Sacrifice deals 5, so it kills a 5-HP unit but not a 6–7 HP one such as a deployed Chewbacca (3/6), Hunter (4/7) or Captain Rex (7/7). Relentless Hunters and Ima-Gun Di attack for 5. Aggressive Negotiations is the one card that reaches 6–7, but only with a full hand: with 4 cards left, Relentless Hunters attacks for 9. There are only two copies, each needs a ready attacker, and against ping decks your hand is usually too small for it. Turning the Tide needs five or more friendly units to deal 5, and you rarely keep that many against a deck that removes units.
  - **Your attacks bounce off:** apart from Hunters and Ima, your attackers have 1–3 power, and a 5–7 HP body survives two or three of them. Each unit you leave alive kills one of yours on its next attack.
  - **Game 137:** the opponent played ten units with 4–7 HP. You killed six, but three of those kills cost you a unit in the trade, and four of their units were still in play when you conceded. From round 5 on you had at most two units out.
- **Sweepers that scale:**
  - **Pings:** the 4- and 5-HP units shrug off pings.
  - **Real board wipes still kill everything:** Pre Vizsla defeats up to 6 total HP, and The Legacy Run deals 6 divided.
  - **Topple the Summit:** it deals 3 to each damaged unit, so any unit that has taken a ping dies to it. See "Against Boba Fett."
  - **Small units die to anything:** Troublemaker (2), Adamant Ewoks (2), Benthic (2), the Wicket unit (3), Ima-Gun Di (3) and Beast tokens (3).
  - **Refill:** Wicket's draw, the Cannon's When Played draw and Ant Droid's When Defeated draw. Ant Droid is the only one a sweeper feeds. Beast Lair refills the board itself: a sweeper can't touch it, so it makes a new 3/3 every round after the wipe as long as you have a card to discard.
- **Almost no ground Sentinel:** you can only race on the ground.
- **Little Hidden:** only Village Troublemaker has it, and nothing gives it to other units. Deployed Wicket and your 4–5 HP Ewoks can be attacked the turn they land.
- **No healing:** your base can't recover from early damage, so a race you fall behind in stays lost.
- **No late game on an even board:** see the game-stage plan.
- **Blank cards against decks with no ground units:**
  - **Need enemy ground units:** the Wicket unit (it can't attack bases), Benthic's ping and Archers' Ambush.
  - **Need the opponent to damage your units:** Logray.
  - **Needs expensive enemy units:** Wicket's leader-side draw.
- **Shields:**
  - **Damage cards:** Turning the Tide, Reckless Sacrifice and each Cannon shot are one instance of damage. A Shield soaks all of it.
  - **Dorsal Turret:** its defeat needs combat damage to land, so a Shield stops that too.
  - **Answers:** the Saboteurs (Troublemaker, Hunters, Wicket the unit) defeat the Shield first, and Kaydel comes in from the sideboard.
- **Upgrade removal:** Outer Rim Constable and similar cards defeat Heavy Ion Cannon or Beast Lair. The Cannon is still worth 3 resources if it fired once and drew a card. Beast Lair has paid for itself once it has made one Beast.
- **Hand pressure:** the Cannon, Beast Lair, Reckless Sacrifice and Aggressive Negotiations all want spare cards. Against ping decks your hand is 2–4 cards, so you can't feed all of them. Decide each round which one gets the spare card.

### 5. Matchups

- **Fast aggro: even.**
  - **Why it's even:** the 4–5 HP units trade well with 2- and 3-drops. Ima-Gun Di kills any of their early units in one attack. Logray punishes decks that attack your units, and the Cannon kills their 2-HP attackers.
  - **Beast Lair is slow here:** it costs a card and a round's tempo before the first Beast. Play it only once you're holding off their early units.
  - **Racing:** you lose a pure race to anything faster.
- **Midrange (Tarfful, Sentinel walls): even.** These games go long and grind your units down.
  - **Get past Sentinels:** Cloud-Rider's base damage, plus Saboteur on Troublemaker and Hunters.
  - **Wicket:** hold him on the leader side and attack up each round for the draw.
  - **Beast Lair:** at its best here. Your hand stays full, so the discard is free, and a fresh 3/3 every round keeps your board even.
  - **Clock:** their cards outclass yours after turn 8.
- **Ping / sweeper control (Maul, Old Master): unfavourable.** See "Against Maul" below.
  - **How they win:** Maul plays a unit for 1 less and defeats it right away, so The Legacy Run's 6 divided damage lands for 4 resources. Chimaera, Anakin, Pre Vizsla and a Maul that replays units follow from turn 5.
  - **What carries it:**
    - The 4–5 HP units mean each sweep kills fewer of yours.
    - Saboteurs get past Imperial Armored Commando and Jod Na Nawood.
    - Adamant Ewoks, Nightfall, Y-Wing and the Cannon deal damage that a sweep can't undo.
    - Ant Droid draws a card whenever their pings or sweeps kill it.
    - Beast Lair makes a new unit every round after a sweep.
- **Indirect damage / ping (Boba Fett): unfavourable.** See "Against Boba Fett" below.
  - **How they win:** they ping every unit with Mortar Trooper, IG-2000 and Justifier, then Topple the Summit kills every damaged unit. Boba deploys as a pilot at 6 resources onto a ship you can't kill. Planetary Bombardment and General Grievous finish the base.
  - **What carries it:**
    - Dorsal Turret for Boba's ship.
    - The Cannon for their Droid Missile Platforms and Constables.
    - Ima-Gun Di, which kills Mortar Trooper (1/4) in one attack.
    - Keeping your units undamaged when Topple is coming. Beasts enter play undamaged, so a Beast made at the start of the round Topple lands is safe from it.
    - From the sideboard, Saw Gerrera (see "Sideboard").
- **Slow ramp (few early plays, big ships): favourable.**
  - **Why:** you get several free rounds of base damage before their threats land.
  - **Removal:**
    - Reckless Sacrifice (discard a 1-drop) deals 5 to their first big threat.
    - Dorsal Turret kills a big ship.
    - Turning the Tide or a Nightfall attack handles the next one.
    - The Cannon finishes whatever is left damaged.
  - **Draws:** cheap units attacking up draw cards.
- **Ground midrange with big units (Chewbacca): unfavourable.** See "Against Chewbacca, Relentless Rebel" below.
  - **How they win:** from turn 2 nearly every unit they play has 4–7 HP. Many fight when they are played (Latts Razzi, Blue Leader's Ambush, Hunter, Captain Rex). Chewbacca's leader action then lets one unit attack again every round. You lose a unit every round without them losing a card.
  - **What carries it:** Adamant Ewoks, Reckless Sacrifice and the Cannon (damage that lands when played), Ima-Gun Di to kill a 5-HP unit in one attack, Aggressive Negotiations to kill a 6–7 HP unit when your hand is full, Nightfall used to finish a unit, Beasts that trade with their 3–4 HP units, the Saboteurs against Hunter's Shields, and your space units hitting their base unopposed.
  - **What doesn't:** Dorsal Turret (they have no big ships) and Turning the Tide (your board is never wide enough).
- **Space / pilots (Luke Skywalker, Wedge Antilles): slightly unfavourable.** See "Against Luke Skywalker" below.
  - **Blocking:** Outland Protector blocks a 7-power pilot ship for 1 resource, and V-19 blocks as well.
  - **Removal:** Dorsal Turret kills the ship. Turning the Tide kills an unpiloted 4-HP fighter. The Cannon kills 2-HP fighters and finishes damaged ones.
  - **Air Superiority:** it only works if they control more space units than you, so play your ships early.
  - **Sideboard:** bring in Shoot Down and the third Dorsal Turret.
- **Leader-combo / Force: even.** You can kill a deployed leader several ways; otherwise race it:
  - Reckless Sacrifice, discarding a 1-drop;
  - Hunters' Saboteur;
  - Turning the Tide;
  - a Dorsal Turret ship, against space leaders;
  - a Nightfall attack.

## How to pilot it

### Mulligan

- **Keep** any six with two cheap units that can damage a base or hold a board. Ant Droid, Logray and the Wicket unit don't count toward the two.
- **A mulligan is all-or-nothing.** It replaces the whole hand, and you must keep the new six (p. 22).

### Resources

- **Setup:** you see the opponent's leader and base before you choose your two setup resources. Use that.
  - **Against Luke, Wedge, Boba or Maul:** never resource Reckless Sacrifice, Dorsal Turret, Outland Protector or Heavy Ion Cannon at setup.
  - **Against a space deck:** don't resource your Y-Wings either.
- **How many:** stop at 4. Go to 5 only if you hold Relentless Hunters, or need to cast a 3-drop (such as Aggressive Negotiations) and a 2-drop in the same round. With nothing above 4, more resources just empty your hand faster.
- **Spare cards with a Cannon or Beast Lair out:** once you have 4–5 resources and either one in play, a spare card is usually worth more as Cannon fuel (2 damage) or a Beast (a 3/3) than as a resource.
- **Beast Lair at setup:** keep it against Maul, Tarfful and Chewbacca, where the game goes long and the board gets wiped or ground down. Resource it against Wedge: the race is decided before it pays off.
- **What to resource first:**
  1. Spare copies of uniques you already have in play.
  2. Dorsal Turret, against decks with no big ships, such as Chewbacca.
  3. Outland Protector, against decks with no space units. Keep V-19 and Y-Wing, which become base attackers nobody can block.
  4. The Wicket unit, Logray and Ewok Archers, against decks with no ground units.
- **Never resource your last Nightfall.** It is a finisher. Resourcing all three lost one game by 2 damage.
- **Never resource your last Reckless Sacrifice against a deck with big units.** It is one of the few answers to a 6+ HP threat.

### Turn by turn

- **Turn 1:** two 1-drops if you have them. Ant Droid is a strong turn-1 play: it can attack up on turn 2, while Wicket is still on the leader side, for two cards.
  - **Against a space deck:** play Outland Protector or Y-Wing, so your ships are already out when theirs arrive.
  - **No 1-drops:** play Cloud-Rider Veteran or Y-Wing, which start chipping on turn 2, or Adamant Ewoks (1 to the base).
  - **No attacks:** units enter play exhausted, so nobody can attack on turn 1.
- **Turn 2:** a 2-drop plus a 1-drop.
  - **Default body:** Ewok Brigade, or Adamant Ewoks when its ping kills something.
  - **Chirpa:** best once two Ewoks are out. He is a 1/5, so he is also the default body against ping decks once he hits for 3 or more.
  - **Beast Lair:** play it on turn 2 only if you already have two units out. Otherwise play bodies first; it does nothing the round it lands.
  - **Against space decks:** V-19 and Archers are 3-drops. Play V-19 early, so it is already guarding when their ships attack.
- **Turn 3:** attack a more expensive unit with a small one and exhaust Wicket to draw. Then deploy him and attack with him for a second draw. Play Heavy Ion Cannon on turn 3 only if you have no better unit to play. Ima-Gun Di is the best turn-3 unit when a 5-HP ground unit needs to die.
- **Turns 4–6:**
  - **Play your hand:** play most of your hand and attack with everything. Cloud-Rider and Y-Wing attack every round.
  - **The Cannon and Beast Lair:** a Cannon plus a 1-drop, or Beast Lair plus a 2-drop, is a good 4-resource turn.
  - **Against a sweeper deck:** keep two units in hand (see "Playing around sweepers").
- **Turn 7+:** count damage. Close with Nightfall on Cloud-Rider, Y-Wing, Hunters or a Saboteur, and use the Cannon each round on whatever blocks.

### When not to deploy Wicket on turn 3

- **Deploy at once** if the opponent has no units, or only 1-cost ground units (pilot decks). The leader side draws nothing then.
- **Hold him** if their board would kill a 2/5 for free and they keep playing expensive units, because the leader side keeps drawing safely. Do this against midrange: in one game he was deployed into Obi-Wan and a Beast, drew one card and died to the next action.
- **Against Maul or Boba**, he can't dodge Anakin's -3/-3 (twice), The Legacy Run, Fennec Shand or Topple the Summit. Deploy him when he can attack straight away, so he draws at least once.
- **He has no protection the turn he lands.** Nothing in the deck gives him Hidden, so any ready enemy unit can attack him right after he deploys.
- **If he is defeated,** he flips back exhausted and can't redeploy (3.4.5, p. 16). His leader-side draw works again from the next round.

### Card-specific lines

- **Hidden** only stops attacks, and only in the phase the unit was played. It doesn't stop damage from abilities or events, which is how the ping decks kill Ewoks.
- **Village Troublemaker:** with your Endor base it always has Hidden and Saboteur. It is the cheapest way past a Sentinel, and a strong Nightfall target.
- **Wicket the unit:** it can't attack bases. Use it to snipe behind Sentinels (Saboteur) and to attack up for draws.
- **Ant Droid:**
  - **The two-card attack:** attack a unit that costs 2 or more. Wicket's leader-side draw triggers when the attack is declared, so you draw before damage. If the defender has 2 or more power, Ant Droid dies and its When Defeated draws again.
  - **Only one Wicket draw a round:** you exhaust Wicket to draw, and any 1-drop qualifies, so take the Wicket draw with Ant Droid only when it would also die. Otherwise attack up with a different 1-drop.
  - **After Wicket deploys:** the leader-side draw is gone, so Ant Droid is a 1/2 that trades into a card. Deployed Wicket still draws on his own attack.
  - **With Logray out:** Ant Droid costs 1, so when it takes combat damage Logray pings an enemy unit for 1.
  - **When they won't kill it:** a defender with 1 power doesn't, and opponents rarely spend removal on it. Then it is a 1-power attacker and good fuel: a 1-cost discard for Reckless Sacrifice hits anything costing 2 or more, and it can be spent on the Cannon.
  - **Against sweepers:** commit it first. Every unit you lose to The Legacy Run, Topple or a ping gives nothing back, except Ant Droid.
  - **It is not an Ewok:** it doesn't pump Chirpa.
- **Outland Protector:**
  - **What it does:** a 1-cost space Sentinel (7.5.11, p. 32). Its job is to soak one hit from a pilot ship or a big capital ship.
  - **Why the block holds:** the enemy ship's On Attack ability can kill it before combat damage, and the attack still deals nothing, because the defender has left play (6.3 step 4.c, p. 26). The only exception is an attacker with Overwhelm.
  - **When to play it:** against ping decks, hold it until there is a big ship to block. A Protector played on turn 2 against Boba died to two pings before any big ship arrived.
  - **Other uses:** it is a Vehicle, so it can carry a Dorsal Turret. It protects your Y-Wings, counts toward Turning the Tide and Archers' Ambush, and keeps Wicket's deployed draw live.
- **Dorsal Turret:**
  - **Targets:** put it on a ready Y-Wing, V-19 or Protector and attack the biggest enemy ship. Any combat damage defeats it: a Luke- or Boba-piloted ship, Chimaera, Nightbrother.
  - **HP doesn't matter:** the defeat is not damage. Your ship usually dies too, which is a good trade.
  - **Leaders:** a ship carrying a leader pilot is a leader unit, and the Turret still defeats it. That makes it your only clean answer to Luke or Boba as a pilot.
  - **Shields:** a Shield prevents the combat damage, so the defeat never triggers. Remove the Shield first.
  - **The Legacy Run:** don't use it on The Legacy Run while you have units in play, because its When Defeated deals 6 damage divided among your units.
  - **Timing:** a ship played this round is exhausted. Keep the Turret in hand until a ship has survived a round, then play it and attack in the same action. A Nightfall attack with a Turret ship also works.
  - **No Vehicle:** an upgrade with no eligible unit goes to the discard pile (6.2 step 5.b, p. 25), so don't play it without one. A stranded Turret is good Cannon fuel.
- **Heavy Ion Cannon:**
  - **What it is:** a Fortify upgrade. It attaches to your base, not a unit (7.5.21, pp. 34–35), and draws a card when played.
  - **The action:** once each phase, discard a card to deal 2 damage to any unit in either arena.
  - **Two copies:** each copy gives the base its own copy of the ability. On a plain reading, two Cannons give two shots per phase, but the rules don't cover this case directly, so check the first time it comes up.
  - **What it kills alone:** units with 2 HP or less, such as Droid Missile Platform, Outer Rim Constable, and other 2-HP units in either arena.
  - **What it finishes:** after one ping or attack, it finishes 3-HP units such as Fennec Shand, Third Sister, Zygerrian Starhopper and Jod Na Nawood. Against a big ship, it adds 2 to a ship your attacks have already damaged.
  - **What to discard,** best first:
    1. A spare copy of a unique already in play.
    2. A Dorsal Turret with no Vehicle to carry it.
    3. An Outland Protector against a deck with no big ships.
    4. A 1-drop late in the game.
  - **Beast Lair wants the same cards.** See Beast Lair below for how to split them.
  - **What not to discard:** your last Nightfall, or your last Reckless Sacrifice against big units. Reckless Sacrifice also needs a spare unit to discard, so keep one for it if you hold it.
  - **Timing:**
    - **Before your attacks:** shoot a blocker or a 2-HP Sentinel to open the way.
    - **After your attacks:** finish a unit they damaged.
    - **Your own units:** don't shoot your own units to trigger Logray. A card for a 1-damage ping is a bad trade.
  - **Cost of use:** each shot is an action and a card. With a 2–4 card hand, expect to fire it about once a round.
- **Beast Lair:**
  - **What it is:** a Fortify upgrade. It attaches to your base, not a unit (7.5.21, pp. 34–35).
  - **The trigger:** when each action phase starts, before anyone acts, you may discard a card. If you do, you create a Beast token: a 3/3 ground unit with the Creature trait and no aspect icons (3.7.17, p. 18).
  - **Timing:** it does nothing the round you play it. The first Beast arrives at the start of the next round and enters play exhausted (1.4.b, p. 5), so it first attacks the round after that. A Lair played on turn 2 gives a Beast that attacks on turn 4.
  - **What a Beast turns on:** tokens count as costing 0 (3.7.2, p. 18). So:
    - Wicket's leader-side draw triggers when a Beast attacks any enemy unit that costs 1 or more.
    - Deployed Wicket's draw stays live, and so does Ewok Archers' Ambush.
    - Logray pings when a Beast takes damage.
    - Each Beast counts for Turning the Tide.
  - **What it doesn't turn on:** a Beast is not an Ewok, so it doesn't pump Chirpa. It is not a Vehicle, so it can't carry a Dorsal Turret. It is created, not played (3.7.2), so it triggers nothing that cares about playing a unit.
  - **Sharing spare cards with the Cannon:** you choose the Beast Lair discard at the start of the round, before you know what the round brings. With only one spare card, keep it for the Cannon if a 2-damage shot kills something this round. Otherwise make a Beast. Use the same discard order as the Cannon, and never discard your last Nightfall, or your last Reckless Sacrifice against big units.
  - **Aggressive Negotiations:** each discard makes it 1 weaker. Skip the Beast in the round you plan to cast it.
  - **Against sweepers:** get it down before the sweep. It is the one part of your board that a sweeper can't reach, so it rebuilds one 3/3 a round.
  - **Against Topple the Summit:** a new Beast is undamaged, so a Topple that round doesn't hit it.
  - **Removal:** an ability that defeats upgrades can remove it, such as Outer Rim Constable. A defeated Beast token is removed from the game, not discarded (3.7.3, p. 18).
- **Ima-Gun Di:**
  - **What it is:** a unique 3-cost 5/3. Its three aspects (Command, Aggression, Heroism) are exactly what Wicket and Bright Tree Village provide, so it costs no penalty.
  - **What it kills:** any 5-HP ground unit in one attack: Captain Typho, Obi-Wan, Gungi, Blue Leader on the ground (a trade), Mortar Trooper, and The Mandalorian once his Shield is gone.
  - **Finisher:** Nightfall makes it a 7-power attacker. Aggressive Negotiations with 4 cards left makes it 9.
  - **Draws:** at cost 3 it draws off Wicket's leader side when it attacks a 4-cost or bigger unit, and Logray pings when it takes damage.
  - **Fragile:** 3 HP. Luke's ping plus Red Five, The Legacy Run's split and Anakin's -3/-3 all kill it. Ping decks will aim at it first, which costs them a removal effect your 4–5 HP units don't have to face.
  - **When Defeated:** you usually have fewer resources than the opponent by turn 5, because you stop at 4–5. If so, you may resource a card from your hand, such as a spare unique, and then the top card of your deck goes to your resources too. This is a small bonus, not a reason to play it.
- **Adamant Ewoks:** the Endor base means the ping always works, even on an empty board. It has been one of the best cards in the games played, because its damage lands when it is played and can't be answered first.
- **Logray:**
  - **The trigger:** every time one of your cheap units takes damage, you ping. Beasts count: tokens cost 0. That includes damage from The Legacy Run, Luke's ping, Red Five and Mortar Trooper, so the more of your units survive, the more you ping.
  - **When he is strong:** against decks that damage your units (five pings in one game). He is blank against decks that only attack your base.
  - **Against Topple the Summit:** don't put indirect damage on your own units to feed him, because every damaged unit dies to Topple.
- **Ewok Brigade:** a plain 2/4 Ewok. It survives Luke's ping plus Red Five's 2, and on a 4-HP board The Legacy Run's 6 divided damage kills at most one unit.
- **Ewok Archers:**
  - **Ambush:** with another unit costing 3 or less out, it can attack an enemy unit the turn it is played (7.5.5, p. 31). It is still exhausted afterwards, and it can't Ambush a base.
  - **Best use:** kill 2-HP ground units, or finish a damaged one.
  - **Toughness:** at 5 HP it survives every ping and Legacy Run's split.
- **Chief Chirpa:** +1 power per other Ewok unit, including deployed Wicket. Ant Droid, Benthic, Ima-Gun Di, Cloud-Rider, Y-Wing, V-19, Protector, Hunters and Beast tokens don't count. There are 19 Ewoks, so he usually hits for 3–5 by turn 4, and his 5 HP survives every ping and The Legacy Run's split. Play him once two or three Ewoks are out. He is unique: if you draw the second copy while the first is in play, resource it or use it as Cannon or Reckless Sacrifice fuel. Playing it would force you to defeat one of the two (1.16.b, p. 11), which only makes sense if the one in play is about to die anyway.
- **Benthic:** one copy. The attack ping hits ground units only. His 1 base damage on defeat makes him a fine unit to trade away.
- **Cloud-Rider Veteran:**
  - **Base damage:** the 2 base damage happens whatever it attacks, so a Sentinel doesn't stop it.
  - **Attacking up:** attacking a unit that costs 3 or more gets the base damage and Wicket's leader-side draw.
  - **Best attacker:** it was the best attacker in the games played.
  - **Weak spots:** ping decks target it first. Its 4 HP survives one ping, but not Air Superiority (4 damage) or a ping followed by Topple the Summit.
- **Red Squadron Y-Wing:**
  - **Indirect damage:** the opponent splits the 3 indirect damage between their base and units, and it can't be prevented (8.35, p. 47).
  - **What to attack:** attack the base unless a space unit must die. Attacking the base deals up to 4 a turn (1 combat plus 3 indirect). Expect less: opponents often put the damage on units, and a 1-cost space Sentinel or an Ambush ship stops it.
  - **Turret carrier:** it is the cheapest Dorsal Turret carrier.
- **V-19 Skirmisher:**
  - **Sentinel:** it counts itself toward the three units, so it becomes a Sentinel once two other units are out (7.5.11, p. 32). Count your units before relying on it: with only two units in play, a Boba-piloted Justifier went straight past it to the base for 8.
  - **Against decks with no space units:** it is a 3-power base attacker that can't be blocked on the ground.
  - **Toughness:** at 3 HP it dies to a pilot ship's On Attack, so it buys one turn at most.
- **Reckless Sacrifice:**
  - **Targeting:** the target must cost more than the unit you discard. A discarded 1-drop hits anything costing 2 or more. Discarding Hunters only hits units costing 5 or more. It never hits a 1-cost unit, so it can't touch 1-cost ships carrying pilots.
  - **Reach:** it can hit space units and deployed leaders.
  - **Fuel:** keep one spare cheap unit in hand while you hold it. A spare unique 1-drop is the best discard.
  - **Stolen AT-Hauler:** if you defeat it, you may play it from their discard pile for free that phase. That is a free 4/5 for you. In game 133 the simulator didn't offer the replay, so check for it.
- **Turning the Tide:**
  - **Damage:** it counts friendly units in both arenas, Beast tokens included, so it deals 4–6 by turns 4–5.
  - **Targets:** save it for a unit you can't remove in combat, such as an unpiloted ship, a Sentinel or a deployed leader.
  - **Shields:** it is one instance of damage, so a Shield soaks all of it.
- **Relentless Hunters:** the Saboteur that can attack bases. Play it the first round you can, or resource it. Holding it for later wasted it in two games.
- **Aggressive Negotiations:**
  - **What it does:** attack with a ready unit; it gets +1/+0 for each card in your hand for that attack. The card has already left your hand, so a hand of 6 when you cast it gives +5.
  - **When it is good:** when your hand holds 4 or more other cards, which happens against midrange that kills your units with attacks rather than pings. In game 139 against Tarfful, the hand held 5–6 cards from round 4 to the end.
  - **When it is weak:** with 2–3 other cards it is +2 or +3 for 3 resources. Against ping and sweeper decks (Maul, Boba, Luke) hands sit at 2–4, so treat it as Cannon fuel or a resource there.
  - **Timing:** cast it before your other cards that round, while your hand is biggest.
  - **Ready attacker:** it is an attack, not an extra attack, so it needs a ready unit that hasn't attacked this round.
  - **Killing a big ground unit:** this is its main job. With 5 cards left, Ewok Brigade attacks for 7 and Relentless Hunters for 10 (Saboteur, so a Shield or Sentinel doesn't stop it). In game 139, that kills deployed Tarfful (3/7) in round 6; Nightfall on the same Brigade only reached 5.
  - **Hitting the base:** Cloud-Rider (+2 to the base on attack), Y-Wing (+3 indirect) or a Saboteur past a Sentinel wall. Deployed Wicket also draws a card on the attack.
  - **Counter-damage:** the attacker still takes the defender's power, so pick an attacker that survives it or one you are happy to trade.
  - **Draws during the attack:** Wicket's leader-side draw and deployed Wicket's On Attack draw add a card to your hand mid-attack. Whether that raises the bonus depends on when the simulator counts your hand. Check it the first time it comes up, and don't rely on the extra point until you have.
  - **Holding cards for it:** once you hold it, a big hand has value. Don't resource a card just because your hand is full, and don't spend your last spare card on the Cannon in the round you plan to cast it. Ant Droid's When Defeated draw feeds it.
- **Nightfall:** you need a ready unit for the attack half. The best targets are:
  - Cloud-Rider (5 to the base in total);
  - Ima-Gun Di (7 power, kills a 6–7 HP unit);
  - Hunters or Troublemaker past a Sentinel;
  - Y-Wing for a second 3 indirect;
  - a Dorsal Turret ship into a big ship.
- **Nightfall against a deck whose units attack again (Chewbacca):** use the 1 damage and the +2 attack together to kill a damaged unit, not to hit the base. In game 137, Blue Leader (5/5) had 2 HP left after Nightfall's ping. A Nightfall-boosted Leia Organa (a 2/3 in that list, 4 power with Nightfall) could have killed it, but she attacked the base for 4 instead. Blue Leader then killed another of your units.

### Initiative

Taking the initiative ends your actions for the round (p. 9), so take it only when you have nothing better left. It is worth more here than in most decks. Units played this round stop being Hidden next round, and acting first lets them attack before they can be attacked.

An opponent who claims it every round will remove your best attacker with their first action each round. Against that, lean on damage that lands when a card is played (Adamant Ewoks, Nightfall, the Cannon), and attack first with the unit most likely to be removed.

### Playing around sweepers

- **Against a deck with The Legacy Run, Pre Vizsla or a similar sweeper:** keep three or four units out, mostly 4–5 HP, and hold two units in hand.
- **What the held units do:** they rebuild the board after the sweep, and they are fuel for Reckless Sacrifice and the Cannon.
- **What keeps pressure on:** Cloud-Rider, Y-Wing and the Cannon keep the clock going on their own.
- **What to commit first:** Ant Droid, because a sweep that kills it draws you a card. Get Beast Lair down before the sweep too.
- **Against Topple the Summit:** the danger is damage already on your units, not the number of units. See "Against Boba Fett."

### Playing around bounce and exhaust

Against decks that return or exhaust your units (Razor Crest, Death Space Skirmisher, Jod Na Nawood), attack with your biggest ready unit before you play cards. A unit that has already attacked loses nothing that round if it is bounced or exhausted.

### Playing Han Solo

- **Advantage tokens:** each gives +1/+0, and each is defeated when the attached unit's attack or defense ends (3.7.15, p. 18). So the +3 lasts for one attack, or until the unit is attacked.
- **Best use:** play Han, put all three tokens on a ready unit that hasn't attacked this round, then attack with it. Relentless Hunters becomes an 8-power Saboteur, Chirpa with three other Ewoks becomes 7. That kills a 6–7 HP ground unit such as Hunter, Captain Rex or deployed Chewbacca.
- **No ready unit:** the tokens can go on Han himself for 6 power next round, but they are lost if he is attacked first.
- **The 3 self-damage:** Han ends at 4 HP left, and stays there: nothing in the deck heals units. Treat him as a 3/4. Logray doesn't trigger, because Han costs 4.
  - **Attacks:** any 4-power attacker kills him in one hit.
  - **Topple the Summit:** he is always a damaged unit, so Topple always hits him. Topple alone leaves him on 1 HP (3 + 3 = 6 damage on 7 HP), but one more ping first (Mortar Trooper, IG-2000, Justifier) kills him (3 + 1 + 3 = 7).
  - **Sweepers:** 4 points of The Legacy Run's 6 kill him.
- **Never give the tokens to an enemy unit.** "A unit" includes theirs.
- **Cost:** at 4–5 resources he is your whole round.

### Playing Saw Gerrera

- **The body:** a 3/6 for 4 that starts undamaged. It is the toughest unit you can bring in.
  - **Topple the Summit:** it only hits him once he is damaged. One ping plus Topple is 4 damage, and two pings plus Topple is 5, so he survives both.
  - **The Legacy Run:** he dies only if they put all 6 damage on him.
  - **What kills him:** Anakin's two -3/-3s, a 6-power attacker, or Topple after three pings.
- **The ramp:** each time the opponent plays an event, the top card of your deck goes to your resources. It costs no card from your hand, and the new resource enters play exhausted (1.4.b, p. 5), so it is usable next round. Extra resources let you play a 3-drop and a 2-drop in the same round, or Han Solo plus a 1-drop. It is a bonus, not the reason to play him: with nothing above 4, the deck has little to spend them on.
- **What he isn't:** not a Saboteur, not an Ewok (no Chirpa pump), and at cost 4 Logray doesn't trigger off him.
- **Unique:** one copy, so there is no spare to worry about.
- **Against Boba:** his best matchup. Their pings don't set him up for Topple, and Topple and Planetary Bombardment are both events, so each one also gives you a resource.

### When to bring in more Aggressive Negotiations

- **The test:** in game 1, count your hand at the start of each of your action phases from round 4 onward. If it reaches 5 or more cards, bring in the third Aggressive Negotiations for game 2. In game 139 against Tarfful it was 5–6 every round from round 4. Before round 4, a hand of 5 is normal and tells you nothing.
- **Why 5:** the card counts the cards still in hand when it resolves, and it has already left your hand by then. A hand of 5 makes it +4, a real finisher. With a hand of 2–4, which is normal against ping and sweeper decks, it is +1 to +3 for 3 resources, and you may want the main-deck copies out (see the Maul and Boba rows above).
- **Which opponents:** a full hand means the opponent kills your units one at a time with attacks, or not at all, while Wicket keeps drawing. That points to midrange with big ground units (Tarfful in game 139), control without pings, or ramp. Ping decks kill several cheap units a round and keep your hand small.
- **What to take out:** the cards that matchup doesn't need.
  - **No big ships:** take out 1 Dorsal Turret.
  - **They do have big ships:** keep the Turrets and take out 1 Benthic "Two Tubes".
- **How to play it:** see Aggressive Negotiations in "Card-specific lines".

The six most recent games (129–134) were all against top meta decks, and they shaped the current list:
- **Abilities killed most of your units.** In games 129–132, every unit killed by an ability had 3 HP or less, so the list has more 4–5 HP Ewoks. In game 133, Topple the Summit then killed four 4–5 HP units at once, because they were already damaged.
- **Big ships went unanswered.** That was a Luke-piloted 7/9, Chimaera and Nightbrother, then a Boba-piloted 4/8 TIE Bomber and 8/8 Justifier. Dorsal Turret is the answer. It was drawn only once in the two Boba games, so the third copy sits in the sideboard.
- **Hands stayed small.** The hand held only 2–4 cards every round, so cards that scale with hand size or need setup were weak.
- **The clock is still too slow.** By round 6 the opponent had taken only 7–10 damage in most of these games.

Game 139, against Tarfful, showed the other side: against a deck that kills units by attacking, your hand stays at 5–6 cards, and the problem is turning those cards into damage. That is why two Aggressive Negotiations are in the main deck.

### Against Maul, Old Master

- **Their engine:**
  - **The combo:** Maul's leader action plays a unit for 1 less and defeats it at once. The Legacy Run then deals 6 damage divided among your units for 4 resources. They did this twice a game.
  - **Replays:** Maul's deploy replays a unit defeated that phase.
- **What follows from turn 5:** Anakin Skywalker (two -3/-3s), Chimaera (defeats a unit, heals their base), Nightbrother (replays a unit from discard) and Pre Vizsla (defeats units totalling 6 HP).
- **Their Sentinels:** Imperial Armored Commando (Shielded) and Jod Na Nawood (who can also exhaust an arena) block the ground.
- **The clock:** Maul deploys at 7 resources and Pre Vizsla costs 8, so they need to be dead by about round 7. In both games they had taken under 10 damage by round 6, which is too slow.
- **How to play it:**
  - **Spread the board:** play 4–5 HP units and never put more than four units out, so one Legacy Run can't wipe you.
  - **Get past Sentinels:** Troublemaker and Hunters (Saboteur) get through Commando's Shield and the Sentinels.
  - **Don't kill The Legacy Run with your own units out.** Kill it when your board is empty, so its 6 damage has nothing to hit (game 129).
  - **Space:** their Onyx Squadron Brute hits your base for 2 each round unopposed. Contest space early with Protector, Y-Wing or V-19, or shoot it with the Cannon.

### Against Luke Skywalker, Hero of Yavin

- **Their engine:**
  - **Pings:** after a Fighter attacks, Luke's leader action pings 1, and Red Five deals 2 to a damaged unit. Together they kill anything with 3 HP or less every round. Hotshot Maneuver, Air Superiority, Green Leader and Resistance Blue Squadron add more.
  - **Pilot ship:** at 6 resources Luke deploys as a pilot. A Resistance Blue Squadron carrying him was a 7/9 that dealt 3 to a unit on every attack and hit the base for 7. The Luke upgrade can't be defeated by enemy card abilities, but the ship under it can.
- **Both games were close** (opponent on 8 and 6), so a few points of blocking or removal swings the matchup.
- **How to play it:**
  - **Before Luke deploys:** get a space unit out early so Air Superiority stays off.
  - **Your 4–5 HP units:** play them so the ping-plus-Red-Five combo needs two turns per kill.
  - **Turning the Tide:** save it for Red Five before Luke boards it. Red Five has 4 HP.
  - **The Cannon:** use it on Red Five or Green Leader once they are damaged, before Luke boards one.
  - **After Luke deploys:** chump the pilot ship with Outland Protector or V-19. Kill it with a Dorsal Turret ship, or with Reckless Sacrifice plus Turning the Tide.
  - **Setup resources:** keep your space cards in hand. In game 131, V-19 and Reckless Sacrifice were resourced at setup, and the Y-Wing and Cloud-Rider later.

### Against Boba Fett, Any Methods Necessary

- **Their engine:**
  - **Pings:** Mortar Trooper (1 damage to each of up to 3 ground units, every round), IG-2000 (1 to each of 3 units), Justifier (1 damage when played and on every attack), Adamant Ewoks and Nightfall.
  - **Boba's leader side:** every time they deal non-combat damage, they can exhaust Boba to deal 1 indirect damage to you.
  - **Topple the Summit:** it deals 3 damage to each damaged unit. It has Plot, so when Boba deploys they can play it from their resources (paying 5) and replace it with the top card of their deck. In game 133 one Topple killed deployed Wicket, Adamant Ewoks, Ewok Brigade and V-19.
  - **Boba as a pilot:** at 6 resources (about round 5) Boba deploys as an upgrade on a ship and deals up to 4 damage divided among units. In these games he made a 4/8 TIE Bomber (3 indirect damage every attack) and an 8/8 Justifier that hit the base for 8 twice.
  - **The finish:** Planetary Bombardment (8 indirect damage), General Grievous (4 to your base when played, an 8/5 attacker, and bases can't be healed), Fennec Shand (Ambush, Saboteur) and Devastator (4 indirect when played).
- **How to play it:**
  - **Keep your units undamaged before Boba deploys.** In the round before they reach 6 resources, put every point of indirect damage on your base, not your units, and don't feed Logray. A unit with 4 HP still dies to Topple if it already has 1 damage.
  - **The exception is Devastator.** While it is in play, they assign all indirect damage they deal to you, so you can't keep it off your units.
  - **Hold Outland Protector** until Boba's ship or Grievous is on the board. Played early, it just dies to a ping.
  - **Save a Dorsal Turret for Boba's ship,** and keep a Vehicle alive to carry it. The ship is a leader unit, so Reckless Sacrifice works on it too, but 5 damage doesn't kill an 8-HP ship.
  - **Keep V-19's Sentinel on.** Have three units in play when their ship can attack, or the 8/8 goes straight to your base.
  - **Shoot the small pieces with the Cannon:** Droid Missile Platform (4/2), Outer Rim Constable (3/1), and Fennec Shand or a Starhopper after a ping. Keep a spare card in hand for it.
  - **Mortar Trooper (1/4) is the engine.** Attack it with Ima-Gun Di or Relentless Hunters (5 power), or ping it and finish it with the Cannon.
  - **Saw Gerrera from the sideboard** is the unit that survives their pings plus Topple. See "Playing Saw Gerrera".
  - **Watch your base total.** Planetary Bombardment and Grievous together deal 12 or more in two turns. When you are at 12 or less, race rather than develop.
- **The clock:** they had taken only 10 and 7 damage by round 6. You need roughly 20 by then to finish before Bombardment and Grievous do.

### Against Wedge Antilles pilots

- **It is a pure race,** and a well-piloted Wedge deck wins it. Pilots stacked on a 1-cost ship hit for 6 to 8 a round from round 2.
- **Removing the ship:**
  - Reckless Sacrifice can't hit it, because it costs 1.
  - Dorsal Turret kills it whatever its HP.
  - Turning the Tide from a wide board can kill it.
  - The Cannon adds 2 after an attack has damaged it.
  - Outland Protector and V-19 with Sentinel absorb hits.
- **Wicket:** their ground units all cost 1, so deploy him on round 3. Only a Beast (cost 0) can attack up into a 1-cost unit for the leader-side draw, and you rarely have one out by then. Resource the Wicket unit and Logray.
- **Attack before playing cards,** because Razor Crest bounces and Death Space Skirmisher exhausts.

### Against Tarfful midrange

- **Their plan:** they kill your units for seven rounds and then turn on your base. Bo-Katan Kryze at 9 resources gives every unit you control -3/-3, so the game must end by round 8.
- **Wicket:** hold him and take the leader-side draw each round by attacking up. All of their units cost 3 or more.
- **Your best attacker:** Cloud-Rider Veteran, because Tarfful and Koska Reeves are Sentinels and its base damage ignores them. The Saboteurs also get through.
- **Nightfall:** save it for the last 6 damage. Game 118 was lost with the opponent on 2.
- **The game 139 list:** Tarfful's leader action makes Beast tokens, and he deploys at 6 resources (round 5) as a 3/7 Sentinel. Their units have 4–7 HP: The Mandalorian (5/4, Shielded), Anakin Skywalker (5/7, -3/-3 when played), Luminara (7/7), Kelnacca, Hunter, Paz Vizsla. Latts Razzi and Blue Leader fight when played. Chimaera and Bo-Katan's Gauntlet are in space. They claimed initiative every round from 1 to 4.
- **Don't deploy Wicket into The Mandalorian.** In game 139 he deployed at the end of round 3, after they had claimed initiative, and the Mandalorian killed him with their first action of round 4. His Shield soaked Wicket's return damage. Stay on the leader side and attack up for the draw; their units all cost 3 or more.
- **The Mandalorian's Shield protects their other units too.** While it is on him, he can defeat it to prevent damage to another friendly unit, so a Cannon shot or Reckless Sacrifice on a different unit can be blanked. Strip the Shield first with a Saboteur. Relentless Hunters (5 power, Saboteur) kills him outright, so don't resource your Hunters in this matchup. In game 139 both were resourced.
- **Kill deployed Tarfful when he is damaged.** In game 139, Nightfall plus Ewok Brigade left him at 3/2, and he then attacked for 3 and made a Beast before he died the next round. One Cannon shot would have finished him. A defeated leader flips back and can't redeploy (3.4.5, p. 16).
- **Aggressive Negotiations is at its best here.** Your hand stays full, so it is +4 or +5 on one attack. Hold it for deployed Tarfful or Anakin.

### Against Chewbacca, Relentless Rebel

- **Their engine:**
  - **Chewbacca's leader side:** Action [2 resources, exhaust]: attack with a unit, even if it is exhausted. It can't attack bases. So a unit they played this turn can attack one of yours anyway.
  - **Deployed Chewbacca:** a 3/6 at 5 resources (round 4 in game 137). His unit side has the same action for free, once each round. In game 137 they used it every round from 3 to 7.
  - **Units that fight when played:** Latts Razzi (deals damage equal to her power to a ground unit), Blue Leader (Ambush; pays 2 to move to the ground as a 5/5), Hunter (4/7; two choices of a Shield token or an attack), and Captain Rex (7/7). Latts killed a 3/3 Ewok, Blue Leader killed deployed Wicket, Hunter killed Leia Organa (then in the list), and Rex killed Relentless Hunters.
  - **Big bodies:** Mina Bonteri and Huyang (4 HP), Captain Typho (4/5 Sentinel), Obi-Wan and Gungi (5 HP), Hunter, Rex, Hijacked AT-ST and King Grakchawwaa (6–7 HP).
  - **Removal and the finish:** Nothing Left to Fear, Lost and Forgotten, Sando Aqua Monster (their Naboo base turns it on), and Bo-Katan Kryze at 9 resources (-3/-3 to every unit you control).
- **The clock:** in game 137 they had taken 12 damage by the end of round 4 and only 2 more after that. Once Chewbacca deploys and their 5–7 HP units land, your ground attacks stop reaching the base. Do most of your damage in rounds 2–4, and plan for the game to end before Bo-Katan in round 8.
- **How to play it:**
  - **Kill units with damage that lands when played.** Adamant Ewoks, Reckless Sacrifice and the Cannon kill without giving their unit a counter-attack. Any unit you leave alive becomes a free attacker through Chewbacca's action.
  - **Finish damaged units.** Use Nightfall's +2 attack to kill a unit, not to hit the base (see "Card-specific lines").
  - **Save Reckless Sacrifice** for a 5-HP unit (Typho, Obi-Wan, Gungi, Blue Leader), or for a 6–7 HP unit that has already taken damage. In game 137 it killed Typho cleanly.
  - **Shields:** Hunter puts Shields on their units. Attack a shielded unit with a Saboteur (Troublemaker, Hunters, the Wicket unit). In game 137, Ewok Archers attacked a shielded Chewbacca, dealt 0, and died.
  - **Wicket:** every unit they play costs 2 or more, so the leader-side draw is live every round. Hold him on the leader side while they have an Ambush or When Played attacker in hand or play. In game 137 he deployed in round 3, drew one card, and died to Blue Leader's Ambush the next round.
  - **Space is nearly open.** Their only ships are three Phoenix Squadron A-Wings (3/2) and any Blue Leader that stays in space. Y-Wing and V-19 can hit their base each round where Chewbacca's big ground units can't block them.
  - **Setup resources:** resource the Dorsal Turrets and Outland Protectors first. Keep Reckless Sacrifice and Nightfall.
